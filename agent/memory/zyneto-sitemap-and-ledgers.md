Three ledgers, and they are not interchangeable.

**`Blog/LEDGER.md` is GENERATED. Never hand edit it.** `tools/blog-visuals/ledger.py render` rewrites it from `Blog/data/content-ledger.json`. It covers **blog posts only** and enforces the cooling-off windows (90 days for a new post or refresh, 60 for internal links, 30 for metadata, hero image or schema). Run `python3 ledger.py check <slug>` before refreshing any post; it refuses inside the window.

**Both hand-maintained ledgers were removed on 2026-08-26 on instruction**, along with the plans and dated audits: the industry-page and case-study programmes are finished and their backlogs were empty. Only the generated `LEDGER.md` remains. Everything below about `STATIC-PAGES-LEDGER.md` and `TECHNICAL-BACKLOG.md` is history, not a live instruction, and both files are recoverable from git history if a rule is needed again. The sitemap mechanics further down still apply.

**`Blog/STATIC-PAGES-LEDGER.md` is hand maintained** and is the one to read at the start of any session about case studies, industry pages, nav or `portfolio.ts`. Created 2026-08-07. It holds the page-by-page state table, production HTTP status, sitemap status, the branch `vikas` commit list, the redirect decisions, a 10 step go-live checklist, and since 2026-08-07 an **Open backlog table in section 2** listing every outstanding item with its blocker.

**Split 2026-08-21 on instruction: that Open backlog is CONTENT ONLY now.** Code, infrastructure and Search Console plumbing moved to **`Blog/TECHNICAL-BACKLOG.md`**, also hand maintained, which carries its own Open and Closed tables. **Item numbers are shared across both files and are never reused**, so a change log entry naming item 80 resolves whichever file the row lives in. **Full change log entries for everything, technical included, stay in `STATIC-PAGES-LEDGER.md` section 7.** Do not file a technical row in the content backlog.

**The "Closed, kept for the record" table in `STATIC-PAGES-LEDGER.md` was deliberately left whole** and still holds technical rows. Splitting it would have meant roughly fifteen arguable calls on rows that read equally as content or as code, and it is archive rather than a task list.

## Standing rule: write it down, every time

**The user instructed on 2026-08-07: always update the ledger with both completed work and open backlog.** Not at the end of a batch, not when asked. This is the memory that survives compaction, so anything only said in chat is lost.

**Reinforced 2026-08-13: "do by default, never ask."** The user had to say "update the ledger" after nearly every task on 12 and 13 August. **Updating it is part of finishing the work, not a separate step to raise, confirm or be prompted for.** Do it in the same turn as the change, without mentioning that you are about to. The only thing that belongs in the reply is the finding, not the bookkeeping.

- **Finished something?** Add a change log entry in section 7 of `STATIC-PAGES-LEDGER.md`, newest first, and remove its row from the Open backlog table. **A row leaves the backlog only by gaining a change log entry.**
- **Found new work, or something got blocked?** Add a row to the Open backlog table with its blocker named.
- **Settled a question, even without writing code?** That is an entry too. Decisions get re-litigated otherwise; the "digital transformation revert REJECTED" and "PostRevision history: KEEP" entries exist for exactly this reason.
- **Blog post content changes do not go here.** They belong in `Blog/LEDGER.md` via `ledger.py note`.
- **Is the new work code, infra or Search Console?** The row goes in `Blog/TECHNICAL-BACKLOG.md`, not the content backlog. The change log entry still goes in `STATIC-PAGES-LEDGER.md`.

Write the *reasoning and the measurement*, not just the outcome. Every useful entry in that file answers "why did we do it this way" for someone with no session context.

## The sitemap trap, verified 2026-08-07

**`zyneto.com/sitemap.xml` is not generated by Next.** It is a rewrite to S3 (`${S3_BASE_URL}/media/sitemap.xml`, declared in `next.config.ts` near line 316), and the object it serves is a **sitemap index** listing exactly two children: `/sitemap_services.xml` and `/sitemap_blogs.xml`.

- `/sitemap_services.xml` is a Next route at `src/app/sitemap_services.xml/route.ts`. It builds from `servicesSitemapEntries`, `solutionsSitemapEntries`, and a **hardcoded array** holding the homepage, `/ai`, `/about-us`, `/contact-us`, `/portfolio`, `/blog` and the three policy pages. 48 URLs live.
- `/sitemap_blogs.xml` is an S3 object written by the backend.
- **The index itself is an S3 object.** Adding a new child sitemap is not a frontend change and needs whoever owns the bucket.

**Consequence: a new static page lands in no sitemap at all unless someone adds it by hand.** As of 2026-08-07, `/case-studies`, `/case-studies/grocery-delivery-oman-al-amri-express` and `/industries/logistics-distribution` are in none, and all three return 404 in production because branch `vikas` is unmerged.

Cheap fix is adding the URLs to the hardcoded array in `route.ts`, which works today with no S3 access, at the cost of the filename becoming a misnomer. Correct fix is separate `/sitemap_case_studies.xml` and `/sitemap_industries.xml` routes plus an edit to the S3 index. Do the cheap one at launch so nothing is invisible; do not defer both.

**Ship the page, the `live: true` flag in `categories.ts`, and the sitemap entry in one commit.** Split across three and one gets forgotten.

**Submit the CHILD sitemaps to Search Console, not the index. Learned 2026-08-21 after losing a day to it.** Resubmitting `sitemap.xml` re-fetches the index and **leaves its children untouched**, so the URL counts do not move and it reads as Google ignoring the request. Submitting each child by its own URL, `sitemap_services.xml` and `sitemap_blogs.xml`, pulled both **within two seconds** and closed a 16-URL gap that had persisted for three days. Check `lastDownloaded` per child, never on the index: the index's `submitted` count is an aggregate of whatever it last read and stays stale long after the children are current. `contents.indexed` reads 0 for every property and is a deprecated field, not a signal.

## Bulk copy sweeps

**The nine hard-won rules live in `STATIC-PAGES-LEDGER.md` section 7, "Lessons that generalise". Read them before any sweep.** They are kept there rather than here because they are operational: back up first, never verify with a grep that needs leading context, verify the checker itself, triage by meaning because not every hit is a violation, never touch client testimonials or slugs, and check what concatenates a string before changing a separator.

The one point worth carrying in memory, because it governs whether a sweep is worth doing at all: **copy sweeps are close to SEO-neutral in both directions.** Settled 2026-08-07 after the user asked. Swapping one adjective for another in body copy neither earns ranking nor costs it, and a proposed revert of "digital transformation" was investigated and rejected by the user. **Do not re-litigate individual word choices or spend a turn defending them.** The value is brand register and the sub-15% AI-detection gate in [[zyneto-content-agent-spec]], not search. What moves these pages is added specifics: the logistics page went 1,113 words to 5,218.

## Service pages: audited, thin, parked to September 2026

Measured 2026-08-07, all 33 live pages. **Bimodal: 26 template-built pages sit between 1,958 and 2,326 words, the 7 rebuilt AI pages sit between 3,016 and 3,937, and the industry page benchmark is 5,218.** Twenty-six pages inside a 370-word band is one template filled in 26 times. The ranked list and the full diagnosis are in task 36 and in the ledger's change log, so **do not re-measure**.

The diagnosis, which generalises: **the thin pages describe the technology, the good pages describe the decision the buyer is making.** "NodeJS uses the V8 engine to compile JavaScript into machine code" is a Wikipedia fact every competitor has verbatim. The test is whether a competitor could paste the sentence on their own site unchanged.
